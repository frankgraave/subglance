Notifications
What this page is. Two questions, in this order: can we reach you, and when should we. Everything above the fold answers the first — channels, their delivery health, and a test button. Routing and timing answer the second. The state engine (internal/state) already decides whether something is real via confirmation and flapping suppression; nothing on this page is allowed to re-litigate that. This page only decides who hears about it.

Proposed kind set: email, slack, webhook, telegram, discord. Email ships today; Slack and Discord are both "post JSON to an incoming-webhook URL" and cost almost nothing on top of the generic webhook, but they deserve their own kind because the field is different (one URL, no method, no signing) and because a user picking "Slack" should not have to know they are configuring a webhook. PagerDuty/Opsgenie are deliberately out: they want dedup keys and incident lifecycle callbacks, which is a separate piece of work.
Why delivery health is on the row and not behind a click. A notification channel that stopped working is indistinguishable from "everything is fine" — the silence is identical. So the last delivery result is part of the row's identity, at the same visual level as the destination. Two derived states matter and they must not share a colour: Failed (red, has fired, did not arrive) and Never fired (dashed meta, no evidence either way). A third, healthy, is green. One meaning per signal.
Send test is the most valuable control here and already exists as POST /api/v1/channels/{id}/test. It is per-row rather than a single page button because you almost always want to verify the one channel you just touched. The result renders in place, stays until the next test, and reports latency on success and the upstream error verbatim on failure — a test that just says "failed" makes you go read the server log, which is exactly the trip this button exists to avoid. Try it: the Telegram channel fails.

Coverage preview
Three models, and the one I would ship.
(a) Attach per monitor — PUT /monitors/{id}/channels, which the API already has. Dead simple, perfectly explicit, and the state lives where you look for it. It also degrades badly: at 40 monitors, adding a new on-call channel is 40 edits, and the first time someone forgets one you get a monitor nobody hears about.
(b) Tag rules — "everything tagged production goes to #alerts". Scales, and expresses intent rather than plumbing. The cost is indirection: to answer "who gets paged for checkout-api" you have to evaluate rules in your head.
(c) Default channel plus exceptions — safe by construction, because the fresh install has coverage from the first monitor. But "exceptions" quietly becomes (a) again.
Recommendation: (b) + (c) together, exactly as drawn — ordered tag rules with a catch-all default — and keep (a) as the escape hatch on the monitor page. The default is what makes this safe: no monitor can ever be silently unrouted, which is the failure this whole page exists to prevent. The tag rules are what make it survive growth. And the coverage panel is non-negotiable: rules you cannot see the effect of are worse than tedium. Note that the union semantics matter — a monitor tagged both production and critical notifies both channels rather than stopping at the first match; first-match-wins is a trap when the rule that wins is the quieter one.
Q for Frank: rules union or first-match-wins? Union is safer and matches how people describe it out loud ("prod goes to Slack, and critical also wakes me up"), but it makes "route this one monitor somewhere quieter" impossible without a mute/exclude concept. First-match-wins makes overrides trivial and silence easy to cause by accident. I lean union plus an explicit per-monitor exclude, but that is a real API decision and I do not want to invent it alone.
Q for Frank: do tags exist as a first-class monitor field yet? This whole design assumes they do, and if they do not, tag routing is a bigger ticket than it looks — tags need to be editable on the monitor page, shown on the dashboard, and probably filterable there too. If tags are out of scope for v1, model (c) alone (default channel plus explicit per-monitor attach) is the honest fallback.

What is deliberately absent. No "notify after N failures", no "minimum duration before alerting", no flap threshold. Those all already exist in internal/state — confirmation (default 2), incident lifecycle, flapping suppression — and duplicating them here would give a user two places to configure the same idea, which is how you end up with a monitor that alerts at 2 failures and a channel that suppresses until 5 and nobody able to explain the silence. The knobs offered here are strictly about reaching a person: which events, how loud, and when the human is asleep. The severity floor is the one borderline case, and it is framed as a floor on an already-confirmed state rather than as a second confirmation step.
Quiet hours must fail safe. "Drop" is offered but is not the default, because dropping an alert is an irreversible decision made by a config field at 03:00. The default is hold-and-digest: you still sleep, and at 07:00 you get one message listing what happened. Recovery notifications during quiet hours collapse into the same digest — a thing that broke and fixed itself overnight is a paragraph, not a phone call.
Q for Frank — the one I feel least sure about: is re-notification while an incident stays open a feature, or is it a machine for training people to ignore SubGlance? The argument for it is real: the first message can be missed, and a 40-minute outage where everyone assumed someone else saw it is a genuine failure mode. The argument against is that a repeating alert for a condition you already know about is exactly the signal people learn to filter, and once they filter this one they filter the first one too. Shown here defaulted off, capped (4 repeats, then it stops by itself), and per channel — a Slack room can tolerate a nudge where email cannot. I would rather ship it off-by-default and capped than not ship it, but I would also accept "acknowledge silences it" as the better answer, which implies an ack concept on incidents that does not exist yet.

Does the log belong here or on Incidents? Both, and they are not the same view. On an incident, "who was told, and did it arrive" is one line of the incident timeline — it belongs there because that is where you reconstruct what happened. The version here is the transpose: grouped by channel health over time, which is how you notice that Telegram has failed every single delivery for three days. The incident timeline would never surface that, because each incident only sees its own one failed send. So: keep a per-incident delivery line on the Incidents page, and keep this channel-centric log here. If only one can ship, ship this one — it is the view that catches a dead channel.
The log is also the strongest argument for retention being explicit: this should be bounded (say 30 days or 5000 entries, whichever first) and say so, rather than growing until someone notices the database.
Alerts are going nowhere.
SubGlance is checking 5 monitors and has no way to tell you when one of them breaks. Every failure it detects will be recorded and shown on this dashboard — and nobody will be told.
Email is the fastest: one address, one test message, done in under a minute.
Why this is the loudest text in the product. The house rule is that an empty state is a fact at headline weight, not an apology — and this particular emptiness is not a neutral "nothing here yet", it is a silent misconfiguration that looks exactly like a working install. So it states the consequence in the headline ("Alerts are going nowhere"), gives the specific number of monitors currently unwatched, and offers the one-minute path first. It is not red: red means something is failing right now, and nothing is failing — this is a gap, not an incident, and reusing the incident colour for it would break one-meaning-per-signal. The weight and the wording carry it instead.
Follow-through: the same fact should appear as a single dismissible line on the dashboard until at least one channel exists and has passed a test. Not a modal; a fact that does not go away on its own.
What this page does not do yet. No per-user notification preferences (this is instance-level, matching the product's single-operator shape today), no escalation chains, no on-call schedule. All three are real features; none of them are the thing standing between a fresh install and a delivered alert, which is what this page is for.
